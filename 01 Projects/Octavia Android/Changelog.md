## 0.3.1 — 2026-08-31

**Re-pairing left the old retry loop alive, hammering her forever.** A real bug, found by
re-pairing against a running host — which is the only way it appears.

A retry is a sleeping thread holding the host, port and credential it was created with.
`disconnect()` cleared a single `wanted` flag, and that does not reach it: the thread wakes
*after* `connect()` has set the flag back to true, sees a live client, and reconnects with
the **old** credential. Every re-pair added another lineage, so the stale ones never died.
Her log showed a refusal every one to five seconds, from several loops at once, and no
amount of entering the right token would stop it.

Fixed with a generation counter bumped on every `connect` and `disconnect`: a callback or a
retry belonging to a superseded attempt returns instead of acting, and `open` cancels any
socket a previous generation still held. Verified — `face connected over socket (2 attached)`
and the refusals stop dead rather than continuing underneath a working connection.

**Worth noting what made this visible:** her host logs every refusal. A client that had
failed quietly would have looked like it was simply slow to pair.

## 0.3.0 — 2026-08-31

**Stage 1: it connects to her, and you can talk to her from the phone.** MINOR — the app
does something now. Proven on the real J7 Pro, not an emulator.

She answered "Hello from the phone. What are you running on today?" from a handset, spoke
the reply on the PC, and both turns arrived back in the transcript. Her log said
`face connected over socket (2 attached)` and `face skipping: viseme, level` — so this is a
**second face beside her desktop one**, which is what Stage 3 built the protocol for.

### What is in it

- **`FaceSocket`** — OkHttp WebSocket, `subscribe` then `ready` on open, exponential backoff
  capped at 15 s. A phone leaves and rejoins networks constantly, and a client that has crept
  up to a five-minute wait looks broken for five minutes after the wifi comes back.
- **`FaceViewModel`** — holds only what she has said. Unknown message types fall through a
  `when` with no complaining `else`, because the protocol requires a face to ignore types and
  fields it does not recognise; new ones are not errors here, just not yet used.
- **`FaceScreen`** — state pill, connection line naming the protocol/model/profile, the
  transcript, captions dimmed further when `tentative`, `overheard` shown faintly and never
  swallowed, and a text field.
- **`Settings`** — host, port, credential in app-private preferences.

### Decisions worth keeping

- **`org.json`, not a generated parser.** The protocol says a face must ignore what it does
  not recognise; defensive `opt*` reads express that directly, where a schema-bound parser
  would turn her adding a field into this app crashing. That is one fewer dependency too —
  **OkHttp is the only third-party library**, because Android has no WebSocket client and
  hand-rolling RFC 6455 to avoid one is a poor trade.
- **The typed line is not shown locally.** She answers `say` with `turn who:"you"` *before*
  she starts thinking, so a local echo would double it. Waiting for hers is also correct with
  more than one face attached: the transcript then shows what was said at the desktop too,
  rather than only what was typed here.
- **`say` carries `text`, not `value`.** `setKey` and `setVoice` use `value` and `say` does
  not; getting it wrong is a message she accepts and silently ignores. Checked against
  `OctaviaSession` rather than the table.

### The development loop, which needs nothing exposed

**`adb reverse tcp:8848 tcp:8848`** points the handset's own loopback at the host's, over
USB. So her socket stays bound to `127.0.0.1`, `RemoteAccess` stays off, no Windows firewall
rule is needed and no elevation — and because the connection arrives at her from loopback,
the per-run token works. Wireguard and the remote key replace only the address, later.

### Not in it, deliberately

No avatar, no microphone. The J7 Pro cannot render a VRM and the protocol cannot carry audio
upstream, so both are later stages rather than shortcuts taken here.

## 0.2.1 — 2026-08-31

**Route (A) chosen and built, in her repo, at v0.20.0.** Her socket serves `wwwroot` at `/`
and her avatars at `/avatars/` on the same port and origin as the WebSocket. Stage 0a is
closed; the reasoning stays in `ROADMAP.md` because the *why* outlives the decision.

Verified against a real browser beside her own WebView2 face — two faces attached at once,
the VRM fetched over HTTP, textures loading, her character on screen.

**The consequence for this repo: the app owns the camera and microphone natively.**
`getUserMedia` does not run on a plain `http://<lan-ip>` origin because that is not a secure
context, so they cannot live in the WebView. This is a better fit for the protocol than the
workaround it replaces — the *app* is the face, and the WebView is only how it draws.

Also confirmed against the real J7 Pro, which is **`armeabi-v7a` only** — 32-bit userspace
despite ARMv8 silicon — with a **192 MB** per-app heap cap and 2.87 GB of RAM. The heap cap
in particular is another reason the face waits for the 11T Pro.

## 0.2.0 — 2026-08-31

**There is an app now.** It does nothing, but it compiles and it is a real Android project.
MINOR: the project itself is the new subsystem.

Scaffolded from the `empty-activity` template: **AGP 9.0.1, Kotlin 2.3.20, Compose BOM
2026.03.01, Gradle 9.1.0, `minSdk 28`, `targetSdk 36`, Java 17.** Package renamed off the
template placeholder to `com.n3ctr0.octavia`. `BUILD SUCCESSFUL in 4m 19s`, a 29 MB debug
APK.

**Android Studio turned out not to be needed, and neither did elevation.** The session was
unelevated, so user-scope installs were tried rather than assumed, and all of them worked:
`adb` (platform-tools 37.0.1), Microsoft OpenJDK 17.0.10, scrcpy 4.1, and — the useful one —
**Google's unified `android` CLI**, which does `create`, `sdk`, `emulator`, `run`, `install`,
`screen` and `layout`. That covers everything Studio was on the list for. The SDK lives in
`%LOCALAPPDATA%\Android\Sdk`, also per-user.

Only Temurin 17 refused: it ships no user-scope installer. OpenJDK covers it, so it was
dropped rather than escalated.

**Route (B) is effectively ruled out** — see `ROADMAP.md` stage 0a. There are *two* virtual
host mappings, and the second one serves her **avatars folder**: `AvatarUrl()` hands the face
`https://octavia.avatar/<file>` and `hello` carries it as a URL. A VRM is user data in a
git-ignored folder, chosen at runtime, so it cannot be baked into an APK. (B) therefore still
needs the host serving over HTTP — it just serves less, and adds a sync script and drift on
top of (A)'s cost. The two were never symmetric.

## 0.1.1 — 2026-08-31

**The test devices, and one of them cannot answer the question everyone will ask it.**

A **Galaxy J7 Pro on Android 9** is the tester; a **Xiaomi 11T Pro** is the destination.
`minSdk 28` follows — the floor of the oldest device actually exercised, which also removes
the compatibility branching around `network_security_config`.

The J7 Pro is a good tester for nearly all of this *because* it is slow: an Exynos 7870 with
eight A53s and no big core makes transport, reconnection, battery and push-to-talk honest,
and a `subscribe`/`skip` policy that survives here survives anywhere.

**It cannot judge the WebView renderer.** A VRM of 28 materials plus the room shader, on a
single-core Mali-T830 with 3 GB of RAM, will not run acceptably — and that is a fact about a
2017 handset, not about the approach. Reading route (A)/(B) or the WebView decision off it
would be reading the wrong instrument, so the face waits for the 11T Pro and everything else
is proven on the J7 first. The app has to be useful with no face at all, which Stage 1
already required for other reasons.

Push-to-talk is no longer a preference but a consequence: `AcousticEchoCanceler` is
per-device and unreliable generally, and is not a plan on this SoC.

## 0.1.0 — 2026-08-31

**The repo, the design, and two prerequisites nobody had noticed.** No code yet, and that is
the point.

Her Stage 13 says the app is the last step rather than the first, and lists four things that
must exist before it. All four shipped in her v0.14.0. So this repo was created expecting
step 5 to be the small one it was promised to be.

**Two more prerequisites turned up the moment the renderer question was answered**, and both
of them live in her repo rather than this one. They are written up in `ROADMAP.md` stage 0.

- **The face page is not served over HTTP.** `wwwroot` reaches the built-in face through
  `CoreWebView2.SetVirtualHostNameToFolderMapping` — a WebView2 feature, not a server. Stage
  13 step 1 opened the socket to the LAN and left the page where it was. An Android WebView
  cannot load `https://octavia.avatar`. Either she serves `wwwroot` from the listener she
  already has, or the assets get vendored into the APK; recommended (A), undecided.
- **The protocol has no audio-upstream message.** `say` carries typed text and `sight` carries
  one JPEG; `listen` toggles the microphone attached to the PC. "Hold a mic button and stream
  the reply" is not expressible in protocol version 1. The escalation is text-first, then
  either on-device recognition or a real `audio` message — the latter being the only one that
  matches "a renderer plus a microphone, not a second Octavia".

**Decided: the phone renders her in a WebView reusing the existing three.js face** rather than
a native Compose avatar. The face is already a web page that speaks the protocol, so this buys
the avatar, the room and the lip sync outright, and future face work lands on the phone
without Android changes. It is also what made 0a visible.

**Toolchain: nothing Android is installed on this machine.** No JDK, no SDK, no `adb`, no
Android Studio. Deferred to an elevated session. `gh` 2.98.0 is present at
`C:\Program Files\GitHub CLI\gh.exe` (not on PATH) and authed as `N3cTr0`.
